# UX principles

1. **I’m on your side.** Every question has a citizen-benefit rationale, exposed through “Why are you asking?”
2. **Ask only what changes something.** Questions are progressive; irrelevant branches are not shown.
3. **Make uncertainty visible.** “Potential tax impact” is distinct from a confirmed saving.
4. **Explain, don’t merely calculate.** A number can reveal its inputs and prototype assumptions.
5. **Protect before progressing.** Readiness is a concrete checklist, not a gamified score. A mismatch produces “Don’t file yet.”
6. **Keep the citizen in control.** Synthetic demo data, scenario switching, accessible controls, and no real-document request.
7. **Use calm, plain Indian English.** The product says “Something doesn’t match,” not “Discrepancy identified.”

The visual system adapts the supplied reference’s pale-blue calm, oversized type, rounded action surface, and spacious mobile-first composition without copying its interface.
